Patient Information and Advocacy

Answers to common questions about care delays and how the No Patient Left Behind fund is working to improve access.

1

Why are emergency department wait times so long?

Canada has the highest emergency department use among wealthy nations. Patients go to the ED because they cannot access care quickly enough elsewhere. When hospitals cannot move admitted patients out of the ED into ward beds, the ED backs up and new arrivals wait hours for care.

2

What does the No Patient Left Behind fund do?

The fund supports health system improvements that reduce care delays. This includes funding projects that introduce accountability frameworks, training staff in efficient care delivery, and backing leaders who drive system change.

3

Can process improvements really reduce wait times?

Yes. Research suggests that improving system efficiency can reduce wait times by 30 to 50 percent. Even small improvements of 2 to 4 percent in hospital flow can eliminate waiting room deteriorations.

4

What is an accountability framework?

Accountability frameworks define which programs are responsible for which patients and set clear expectations for timely care. Without them, the default response to overcrowding is to close the door and let patients queue.

5

How are donations used?

Donations fund annual peer-reviewed grants for health system innovation projects. Grants are awarded at the CAEP conference and range from small toolkits to multi-year improvement initiatives.

6

How can I support the fund?

You can make a tax-deductible donation through our foundation partner. Donors receive an official tax receipt from the Schwartz-Reisman Emergency Medicine Institute. Donate here.
