Our Mission & Terms of Reference

Constitution, mission, objective, funding, governance, and board structure of the Grant Innes No Patient Left Behind Endowment Fund.

Mission

To support the development and implementation of health system innovations that improve patient access to appropriate (right care, right place) care in emergency departments, hospitals or communities. To be recognized as a system improvement catalyst, policy advocate, and trusted information source for health leaders.

Objective

The purpose of the No Patient Left Behind fund is to provide annual peer-reviewed funding awards to develop and implement promising access-related health system innovations, particularly those that incorporate concepts of patient care accountability or accountability framework implementations. High priority grant proposals could fall into several categories (see Funding priority areas).

Funding

Funding would arise through the No Patient Left Behind endowment fund. Initial funds would come from our primary benefactor and from a donation program. The fund will award annual system improvement grants that are divisible based on the quality and potential impact of the applications, as determined by the board or a grant review committee. Successful grants would be awarded at the Canadian Association of Emergency Physicians (CAEP) annual conference; they would acknowledge the SREMI/CAEP/No Patient Left Behind collaboration and specifically name the Grant Innes No Patient Left Behind fund and its mission. Growing awareness of the fund through CAEP, SREMI, the oversight committee, and a giving campaign will hopefully lead to ongoing donations from the community.

Primary Benefactors for the No Patient Left Behind fund: Linda and Tom Stevenson.

The Oversight Committee

The oversight committee will collectively provide leadership and vision for the endowment fund, set and monitor progress towards goals and objectives, and engage in strategic decision-making for the fund. They will determine how and to whom annual awards are distributed. If necessary, they will modify the fund's endowment policies, award criteria and fund distribution to assure maximum impact.

The committee may choose to form ad hoc expert panels for specific tasks, such as developing a multimedia strategy and a policy resource library to support emergency medicine change leaders. They should establish strategic alliances that involve nursing, allied health and non-emergency medicine physician leaders in supporting system change. Key collaborations will ideally include the Canadian Medical Association, the National Emergency Nurses Association, hospital and health leadership organizations, provincial medical and emergency medicine organizations, community and philanthropic groups, and ministries of health.

The committee will maintain a database of submitted and funded applications, and evaluations of funded projects. They are not responsible for endowment fund investment strategy, asset allocation or fund management, but will receive relevant financial and audit reports from the Schwartz-Reisman Emergency Medicine Institute.

Committee Composition

The committee will have 7 to 11 members. The chair is responsible for supervising other members. The vice-chair will draft board meeting agendas and carry out the duties of the chair if the chair is unable. The secretary will, in collaboration with CAEP and SREMI, schedule board meetings, manage board correspondence, take meeting minutes, and prepare an annual summary report. Sherry Stackhouse will act as Grant Innes' proxy and serve as a permanent board member. Linda Stevenson, our primary donor, is invited to attend and observe committee meetings as a non-voting presence.

The default term for a committee member is 2 to 3 years. Members may rotate off after one term or serve a second consecutive term if re-elected. No more than one third of the board should turn over in a given year. Members will serve a maximum of 5 consecutive years, then must rotate off. Previous committee members who have rotated off are eligible for reappointment after a two year break.

Board Meetings

There will be two yearly meetings. The first is an annual general meeting in May or June, near the time of the CAEP national conference. At each annual general meeting, the committee should approve previous minutes, deal with unfinished business, receive the secretary's annual report, review financial statements, discuss new business and resolutions, elect new members, and review the fund's constitution and bylaws. The second is an application meeting at least one month after the December 1 project application deadline, where the board evaluates and selects award winning applications.
